## 1. What the news actually did to the story

Between the last pass (early July 2026) and now, the 2026 El Niño stopped
being "an event developing on schedule" and became "an event running
faster than its precedents". Three things moved:

| | early July 2026 | 6 August 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Official framing | El Niño present, expected to strengthen | El Niño Advisory; **very strong peak is the central expectation** for late 2026; ~97 % chance it holds into early 2027; WMO expects further intensification through Aug–Oct |
| Far-ocean reading | +1.44 °C, June (monthly) | ≈ **+2.1 °C**, week centred 15 July (weekly index) |
| PNG on the ground | declaration, directives, Jiwaka on Alert | six Highlands provinces under drought **warning**; frost on gardens at Tambul, Gembogl, upper Mendi, Imbonggu, Hela; Oxfam PNG counting ~1.9 M at risk in the Highlands, reporting >3 M nationwide; drought now forecast to run **into Q1 2027**; Parliament told to fund the response from existing budgets |

The important consequence for the dataviz: **the piece's own estimate has
been overtaken.** The analogue band peaked at +2.3 °C (envelope +1.9 to
+2.7) and put July somewhere between +0.4 and +1.6. July's quoted weekly
reading was about +2.1 — above the whole band.

That is not a problem to hide. It is the most persuasive thing that has
happened to this piece since it was written, because the piece's thesis is
*the ocean tells you early*. So chapter nine now says it out loud: the
precedents are a **floor, not a ceiling**. Card 3 was rewritten around
that, the chart carries the cited reading as a ringed marker above the
band, and the caption says "— and 2026 is already above it".

**What I did not do:** invent a July monthly observation. The weekly index
is a different product on a different SST basis, so it is drawn as an
unconnected marker that names itself as a quoted weekly value, and it is
never appended to `nino34_monthly.csv`.

## 3. Changes I'd make next — dataviz and story

**3.1 Bind chapter nine's copy to its data. ✅ Done.** Cards 1–3 hard-coded +1.44,
+0.53, +1.09, +1.18, +0.84, "+2.3 °C in November" and "1997 matches best".
Every one of those goes stale the next time NOAA appends a month, and the
prose block and aria-label repeat them a third and fourth time.
`ScrollScene`'s `flow` snippet does receive `data` — but reading the
numbers from there would render them empty in the prerendered HTML and for
anyone without JS, because scene JSON is fetched lazily as a scene
approaches. That is right for a chart and wrong for a sentence. So the
pipeline now writes `src/lib/generated/now-copy.js`, which the scene and
`+page.svelte` import at build time: every number in the cards, the prose
equivalent, the aria-label, the figure title, the table caption, the
`BigStat` and the colophon comes from it. The month is named from the data
too, so `june_standings` no longer disagrees with copy that says "June".
Re-running the pipeline is now the whole update.

**3.2 Free the y-domain. ✅ Done.** `NinoMonths` hard-coded `[-2.6, 3.05]`.
If this event verifies near +3 — which is where the August model consensus
points — the observed line would have clipped against the top of its own
chart. The domain is now derived from every mark either view draws
(observations, precedents, the envelope, the anchored path, the cited
reading), padded, and rounded out to a half degree so the gridlines stay
stable between rebuilds; the tick labels follow it. It also recovered the
40 % of chart height the fixed domain was spending on empty space below
the deepest La Niña.

**3.3 Add a card that scores our own estimate. ✅ Done.** The chapter ran
four cards and ended on the calendar. It now runs five, with *Marking our
own homework* inserted at position 4 — before the calendar, so the chapter
still closes on the call to act. It states the miss in the estimate's own
numbers (+1.19 expected, +1.56 at the top of the band, ≈ +2.10 read),
names the structural reason (all four analogues were cooler than 2026 at
the anchor month, so their own levels carry that deficit forward), and
draws the anchored path as a lighter dashed line — the first time it
appears on the chart, earned rather than asserted. A new phase-3 layer
marks the estimate's range at that month and the distance up to what was
actually read. Nobody else in a viz competition shows their forecast being
wrong on purpose.

**3.4 Give chapter eight the live hook.** "The watchers" ends at 6 stations
in 2026 and stops. The 2026 declaration is the payoff for that whole
chapter: annotate it — *this network called the event on 1 May 2026, five
months before the forecast peak* — and the monitoring story stops being an
inventory and becomes a demonstration.

**3.5 One freshness surface, not three.** The piece now states an "as of"
date in three places (`scene_now.json`'s `extracted`, the shelf note, the
colophon), maintained by hand and already capable of disagreeing. Put one
`LAST_UPDATED` in the pipeline output, render it in the colophon and as a
small dateline on chapter nine, and let the shelf note read from it.

**3.6 Consider a "since publication" strip.** The live paper trail is now
nine items across four months and reads as a flat grid. Dated as a short
vertical timeline — 1 May advisory → 1 Jun declaration → Jun drawdown →
Jul warning + frost → Aug "very strong" — it becomes a second reading of
the same argument the ONI chart makes, in the language of documents rather
than degrees. That is also the shape that keeps working as the event runs
on to Q1 2027.

## 4. Changes I'd make next — UI

**4.1 Chapter nine on a phone. ✅ Done.** Below
900 px the figure takes the upper band and gets roughly a third of the
viewport; into that go four precedent lines, their four peak labels, the
2026 line and its label, the estimate band and caption, the cited marker,
the axis, the threshold note and two timing brackets. This pass moved the
marker label and the estimate caption into the legend at narrow widths and
un-collided the 2026 endpoint label. The rest is now fixed too, and
without dropping any series — the collisions were between *labels*, not
lines, so all four precedents still draw:

- Below 560 px the four ghost peak labels, the two orientation labels
  ("El Niño ↑ warm water drains east" and its opposite) and the threshold
  caption all hand off to the legend, and the timing brackets drop their
  date ranges to short names.
- The margins come back to the plot at that width: the right margin exists
  only to hold the threshold caption, so it goes from 104 px to 16 px, and
  the top from 58 to 26. That is about 100 px of width and 36 px of height
  returned to a chart that only had ~150 to begin with.
- The legend is now part of the figure's measured body: the SVG sizes
  itself to what is left after it, instead of a four-line legend pushing
  itself out of the body and over the figure's foot.
- The "La Niña, twice — weak" annotation moves above the zero line on
  narrow, where the field is empty, instead of into the year-tick row.

What is *not* fixed: chapter nine's cards are long, and on a phone a long
card overlaps the graphic band it is reserved against. Giving this scene
its own bottom reservation, or trimming the cards for narrow screens, is
the remaining move.

**4.2 The threshold caption needs a lane. ✅ Done.** `+0.5 °C — the El Niño
threshold` was right-anchored at the plot edge, which is exactly where all
four precedents converge on their way down. It now sits in the right
margin, outside the plot entirely, on two short lines — and on narrow, in
the legend.

**4.3 A "live" marker in the chapter rail.** `ChapterNav` treats all eleven
anchors identically. One quiet dot on chapter nine — with the dateline from
§3.5 in the contents panel — tells a returning reader that this is the part
that changes, which is the whole reason the chapter exists.

**4.4 The legend is doing a lot of work now** — five entries at the last
phase, wrapping to two lines on a 1440 px desktop and five compact lines on
a phone, where it costs the chart real height. It reads correctly and
nothing overflows, but it is the next thing that will strain: a two-column
legend on narrow, or folding the threshold entry back onto the gridline
once the plot is tall enough, are the obvious moves.

## 5. Deliberately not changed

- **The analogue method.** The level-based estimate is still what the band
  and the headline dashed path show, because it is what the four precedents
  actually measured. The anchored variant is computed in the pipeline,
  quoted in the copy, and now drawn — but only from the scoring card
  onward, as a lighter dashed line with no band, so the chart never carries
  two envelopes at once. Nothing is tuned by hand in either.
- **The `+1.44 °C` BigStat.** It is the last verified monthly observation
  and it should stay the headline number until a fetched July replaces it.
  Its caption now carries the mid-July weekly reading as the "and still
  climbing" clause.
- **The 1997/2015 paper trail.** Untouched. Its argument — the precedents
  are documented — is only strengthened by the 2026 shelf growing.
