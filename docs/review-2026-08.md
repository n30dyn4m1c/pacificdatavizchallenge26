# Review — 6 August 2026

What changed in this pass, what the event has done since the piece was
written, and what I'd change next. Ordered by how much it would improve
the entry, not by effort.

---

## 1. What the news actually did to the story

Between the last pass (early July 2026) and now, the 2026 El Niño stopped
being "an event developing on schedule" and became "an event running
faster than its precedents". Three things moved:

| | early July 2026 | 6 August 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Official framing | El Niño present, expected to strengthen | El Niño Advisory; **very strong peak is the central expectation** for late 2026; ~97 % chance it holds into early 2027; WMO expects further intensification through Aug–Oct |
| Far-ocean reading | +1.44 °C, June (monthly) | ≈ **+2.1 °C**, week centred 15 July (weekly index) |
| PNG on the ground | declaration, directives, Jiwaka on Alert | six Highlands provinces under drought **warning**; frost on gardens at Tambul, Gembogl, upper Mendi, Imbonggu, Hela; Oxfam PNG counting ~1.9 M at risk in the Highlands, reporting >3 M nationwide; drought now forecast to run **into Q1 2027**; Parliament told to fund the response from existing budgets |

The important consequence for the dataviz: **the piece's own estimate has
been overtaken.** The analogue band peaked at +2.3 °C (envelope +1.9 to
+2.7) and put July somewhere between +0.4 and +1.6. July's quoted weekly
reading was about +2.1 — above the whole band.

That is not a problem to hide. It is the most persuasive thing that has
happened to this piece since it was written, because the piece's thesis is
*the ocean tells you early*. So chapter nine now says it out loud: the
precedents are a **floor, not a ceiling**. Card 3 was rewritten around
that, the chart carries the cited reading as a ringed marker above the
band, and the caption says "— and 2026 is already above it".

**What I did not do:** invent a July monthly observation. The weekly index
is a different product on a different SST basis, so it is drawn as an
unconnected marker that names itself as a quoted weekly value, and it is
never appended to `nino34_monthly.csv`.

## 3. Changes I'd make next — dataviz and story

**3.1 Bind chapter nine's copy to its data.** Cards 1–3 hard-code +1.44,
+0.53, +1.09, +1.18, +0.84, "+2.3 °C in November" and "1997 matches best".
Every one of those goes stale the next time NOAA appends a month, and the
prose block and aria-label repeat them a third and fourth time.
`ScrollScene`'s `flow` snippet already receives `data`, so this is a
mechanical fix — and it converts the re-run in §2 from a copy-editing job
into a no-op. Same for the `+1.44 °C` `BigStat` on `+page.svelte`, and for
`june_standings`, whose key and surrounding copy say "June" while the
pipeline actually computes the standings at whatever the last observed
month is.

**3.2 Free the y-domain.** `NinoMonths` hard-codes `[-2.6, 3.05]`. If this
event verifies near +3 — which is where the August model consensus points —
the observed line will clip against the top of its own chart. Derive the
domain from the data plus the estimate envelope with a little padding, and
round it to whole degrees so the gridline story stays stable.

**3.3 Add a fifth card: score our own estimate.** The chapter currently
ends on the calendar. A fifth card that puts the piece's July estimate next
to what July actually did — "we said +1.2, it came in near +2.1, here's
why: every one of our four analogues started cooler" — would be the most
distinctive thing in the entry. Nobody else in a viz competition shows
their forecast being wrong on purpose. It also earns the "floor, not a
ceiling" framing rather than asserting it.

**3.4 Give chapter eight the live hook.** "The watchers" ends at 6 stations
in 2026 and stops. The 2026 declaration is the payoff for that whole
chapter: annotate it — *this network called the event on 1 May 2026, five
months before the forecast peak* — and the monitoring story stops being an
inventory and becomes a demonstration.

**3.5 One freshness surface, not three.** The piece now states an "as of"
date in three places (`scene_now.json`'s `extracted`, the shelf note, the
colophon), maintained by hand and already capable of disagreeing. Put one
`LAST_UPDATED` in the pipeline output, render it in the colophon and as a
small dateline on chapter nine, and let the shelf note read from it.

**3.6 Consider a "since publication" strip.** The live paper trail is now
nine items across four months and reads as a flat grid. Dated as a short
vertical timeline — 1 May advisory → 1 Jun declaration → Jun drawdown →
Jul warning + frost → Aug "very strong" — it becomes a second reading of
the same argument the ONI chart makes, in the language of documents rather
than degrees. That is also the shape that keeps working as the event runs
on to Q1 2027.

## 4. Changes I'd make next — UI

**4.1 Chapter nine on a phone is the weakest screen in the piece.** Below
900 px the figure takes the upper band and gets roughly a third of the
viewport; into that go four precedent lines, their four peak labels, the
2026 line and its label, the estimate band and caption, the cited marker,
the axis, the threshold note and two timing brackets. This pass moved the
marker label and the estimate caption into the legend at narrow widths and
un-collided the 2026 endpoint label, which helps — but the four ghost peak
labels still overlap each other, the "+0.5 °C — the El Niño threshold"
caption is still run over by the lines, and the two timing brackets still
touch. Options, cheapest first:

- Drop to two precedent lines below ~560 px — 1997 (best match, and the
  drought the piece keeps returning to) and 2023 (most recent) — and move
  the other two into the legend as text.
- Move the peak labels to the right margin as a small ranked key instead of
  direct labels.
- Give chapter nine a taller pinned band than other scenes on mobile. It is
  the payoff chapter and the only one whose graphic carries four series.

**4.2 The threshold caption needs a lane.** `+0.5 °C — the El Niño
threshold` is right-anchored at the plot edge, which is exactly where all
four precedents converge on their way down. Left-anchor it near Jan of the
onset year, where the chart is empty in every phase.

**4.3 A "live" marker in the chapter rail.** `ChapterNav` treats all eleven
anchors identically. One quiet dot on chapter nine — with the dateline from
§3.5 in the contents panel — tells a returning reader that this is the part
that changes, which is the whole reason the chapter exists.

**4.4 Legend is now four items and wraps to two lines** on a 1440 px
desktop at phase 3. It reads fine, but if 3.1 and 4.1 land it is worth
re-checking that the estimate entry and the cited-reading entry stay on one
line each at common widths — they are the two doing the honesty work.

## 5. Deliberately not changed

- **The analogue method.** The level-based estimate is still what the chart
  draws, because it is what the four precedents actually measured. The
  anchored variant (same trajectories, started from 2026's observed level,
  peak ≈ +2.8 °C) is now computed in the pipeline and quoted in the copy,
  but drawing both would put two dashed paths and two bands on a chart that
  already carries six series. Nothing is tuned by hand in either.
- **The `+1.44 °C` BigStat.** It is the last verified monthly observation
  and it should stay the headline number until a fetched July replaces it.
  Its caption now carries the mid-July weekly reading as the "and still
  climbing" clause.
- **The 1997/2015 paper trail.** Untouched. Its argument — the precedents
  are documented — is only strengthened by the 2026 shelf growing.
